During my Spring semester of college, I decided to go abroad and do an exchange program in Osaka, Japan. Many thoughts filled my head -- I would be going alone, I wasn't the best at speaking Japanese, and I would pretty much be on my own figuring things out.

But it turns out I was completely wrong. As soon as I landed in Kansai International Airport, I was greeted by kind Japanese people and really got to take in the reality that would be mine for the next 5 months.

## March

It was cold. I made a few friends in the dorm, and grew closer with other CSULB students that were also doing the exchange program with me. It was my second time in Japan, but this time I would be living as an actual resident here. The dorm started off as quiet -- other foreign students were settling in and the ice was slowly starting to thaw. I had my own room in a 6 person unit; and although it was quite small, it was still nice and cozy considering it was my first time dorming ever.

I met a bunch of friends from around the world -- Switzerland, Netherlands, South Korea, and even some exchange student friends from USA. We all connected very quickly, and we traveled together during the month of March and explored Japan as a group. We went to out first Izakayas, went to Tokyo and embarked on huge shopping sprees, and prepared for the cherry blossoms which was almost in full bloom. Our school semester would start in April, so we tried our best to spend our time as much as we could and explore Japan before all of us would be occupied with school.

## April 

April was full of memories and friendships began to bloom with the cherry blossoms. After our first trip to Tokyo, we quickly headed back to Ibaraki, Osaka and slowly prepared for our upcoming semester. I would occasionally go to Dotonbori and even shop at the big malls for clothes. Me and Weibo began our rock climbing journey, and although I was hesitant at first, I can confidently say that it has definitely been one of the best experiences so far. I slowly began to adjust myself to a new lifestyle; I always rode my bike back and forth to and from campus, and there were days where I would go straight to Kyoto after school was over.

My Japanese began to improve, and I felt comfortable holding conversations with Japanese people. School wasn't stressful, and I would spend most of my time at the FamilyMart by the dorm trying new drinks and snacks. Life was good, and it all still felt surreal to me. The Japanese people at our rock climbing gym taught me many techniques that had helped me improve, and I even learned Kansai-ben (Osaka Slang) from them.

## May 

During the month of May, my friends and I went to South Korea during Golden Week (a three day weekend I think). We explored all around Seoul -- exploring the bustling night life in Hongdae, walking around Myeongdong and eating amazing street food, and even visiting Suwon to explore the culture as well as visiting several famous K-drama spots 😆. We loved the Korean fried chicken (BHC, BBQ chicken, etc), and the Korean BBQ was amazing. We definitely visited all of the touristy spots, though it did still feel quite different from Japan as a country.

Korea felt a lot more westernized and it felt like being in K-Town Los Angeles again, lol. I enjoyed the overall vibes and it was definitely more lively & romantic with the amount of cafes and couples around. After coming back to Japan, many midterms and tests came up so I was occupied with that. I continued to improve my Japanese and learned more about the different perspectives of Computer Science and technology with other foreign students also sharing their viewpoints. I also joined a basketball circle -- also known as a club -- and I was able to play basketball with other students! Luckily I brought my basketball shoes, and the style of play felt more different compared to the usual back in the States. Me, Weibo, Ati, and Liam also went on the hunt for Nintendo 3Ds', and luckily we were all able to snag one each for a good price :) We went to izakayas every other night to celeberate our studies, and overall we enjoyed the lifestyle together, spending core memories and trying new experiences. May was probably one of my favorite months out of all of them ...


## June 

June was full of memories and the weather began to become more humid and warm. I began to become more occupied with school -- I would still occassionally go on day trips alone to Kyoto or just do some more shopping in Osaka. I visited an abandoned tunnel with my friend Max from Switzerland, and that was a great experience. I continued to put in effort towards learning Japanese, and school was my main priority. I also was able to visit Miyazu, which was north of Kyoto.

I continued to rock climb with Weibo, and spend more memories in the MP (multipurpose room) with other exchange student friends. I was also able to visit Kojima Jeans Street in Okayama, where I bought my own pair of Momotaro Jeans, arguably the best Japanese denim and if not the best in the world.

## July

July was hot. Like I mean really hot. Me and my exchange student friends went to a Japanese Costco for the first time in preparation for an event that we would be hosting on July 4th (American Independence Day), and (yes, in Japan) to show off US culture to Japanese students at my university. It was a great time as I made American food and brought American snacks during the event, as well as talk about American culture to everyone.

After our USA July 4th event, it was finally time to cross off a bucket list item -- climbing Mt. Fuji, Japan. Me, Ati, Liam, Weibo, and Versche took a night bus to Tokyo, and spent around 5 days in Tokyo, before our Fuji summit. We had lots of fun playing arcade games, and overall spending time all around parts of Tokyo since we pretty much only staying in the Osaka and Kyoto regions. 

Honestly, Mt. Fuji was one of the best experiences of my life. It was definitely quite a challenging hike due to the high altitude and uphill battle, but I pushed through with Liam and Weibo and made it to the peak -- those were beautiful memories I will always hold in my heart, especially after just being friends and meeting each other the past few months.

When we got back to Ritsumeikan University in Osaka to finish school, I felt a rush of emotions. I really couldn't believe that school was pretty much done, and the friends I met there would all be going away. I miss my Japanese teacher, Midori Sensei so much -- she really challenged me to improve my Japanese writing and speaking skills, and she played a really big part when it came to my overall confidence and grammar when speaking to strangers.

When school ended, I went on many summer festivals -- in Kyoto, Osaka, and other cities. I ate a lot of street food. 

During late July, I went to Hanoi, Vietnam for the first time. The food there was so good, and it reminded me of the Philippines. The weather was humid, but I love the authenticity of Vietnamese people and the amazing food that they have. I hope to visit Vietnam again and explore more, I miss it!

## August

During August (my last and final month), I did a variety of things: I went to Osaka Expo for the first time and visited so many countries' exhibits, visited Shirakawa-Go which is north of Nagoya, and visited my friends in South Korea. I began to say my goodbyes as Ati had already went Home, Weibo was staying in Vietnam, and Liam was solo traveling on his own.

It was a mix of sadness but enjoyment not at the fact that it was over, but that I was able to really experience the past 6 months living abroad making unforgettable friendships and fulfilling experiences.
